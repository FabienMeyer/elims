# Evidence quality

Do not guess. Calculate deterministic claims and evaluate empirical claims from
evidence. When evidence is incomplete, state the uncertainty, assumptions, and
method required to resolve it.

- Calculate facts that can be derived exactly and show the relevant inputs.
- Measure or observe empirical behavior with a reproducible method.
- Evaluate judgment-based questions against explicit criteria and cite the
  observations supporting each conclusion.
- Label estimates and predictions. State their inputs, assumptions, uncertainty,
  and a method for verification.
- Distinguish absence of evidence from evidence that a condition is absent.
- Do not use test counts, coverage percentages, builds, another agent's verdict,
  or phrases such as `should work`, `negligible`, `intuitive`, or `user-friendly`
  as proof by themselves.
- Record contradictory evidence, skipped checks, environmental limitations, and
  residual risk. Do not silently select the evidence that supports a preferred
  conclusion.

Use evidence proportionate to the claim and risk. A deterministic check is
preferable when one exists. When only qualitative evidence is possible, preserve
the observation and the evaluation criteria separately.
